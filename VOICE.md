# How Simon writes

A working guide for posts on this site, built from the posts Simon wrote or approved.
Read it before drafting anything that goes out under his name. Update it whenever he
corrects a draft, so it gets closer each time.

Sources so far: the phantom power post (2017), DRAC (2018), the first site posts and the
Corne write-up (2025), the sand table build log, the AI post and the MadRP2040 case post
(2026), and his comments in review tools.

## The short version

Write like an engineer telling a mate what they built over a beer. Plain words, short
sentences, real numbers, and own up to what didn't work.

## Tone

- **First person, casual, Australian.** "Threw one together", "dead easy", "did the job",
  "sorted it", "heaps", "a mate". Australian spelling: realised, colour, aluminium, metre.
- **Dry humour, used lightly.** One line now and then, never a running gag. "Last thing you
  want is to cook a brand new mic." "The tweeters cook, and the neighbours come round."
- **Honest about status.** Say what's untested, what broke, what got worse. "The acoustic
  numbers are messier." "None of this has been built yet, so a lot is unproven." No selling.
- **Gives credit by name.** The people, the company, the open source project it's based on.
- **Not precious.** "Took me an embarrassed afternoon." Mistakes are part of the story.

## Sentences

- Short. Often a fragment. "Two stages, nothing clever." "Too slow." "It can."
- Lead with the point, then the detail. No warm-up paragraph.
- One idea per paragraph. Two to four sentences is normal.
- Numbers with units, every time: "0.3 mm per side", "75 cm", "about 2.5 kg of PETG".
  A number beats an adjective.
- Problem, then cause, then fix. "Too tight and fragile. The slot gap goes from 0.1 to
  0.35 mm."

## Structure

- Short posts: no table of contents, a few `##` headings, done.
- Long posts: a table of contents, then **Overview** first, then the story in build order.
- Bullets for parts, mods and lists of findings. Bold the lead word of each bullet.
- A table when there are real numbers to compare.
- Ends with a short "what's next" or "final thoughts", then `-SM` on its own line.
- Build logs grow an **Updates** section at the bottom, newest last, each with a date.

## Words to avoid

These read as AI and Simon hates them. Don't use any of them in posts.

- "canonical", "robust", "seamless", "comprehensive", "leverage", "delve", "showcase",
  "game-changer", "journey", "unlock", "elevate"
- "It's worth noting", "Note that", "In this post we will", "Let's dive in"
- "Watch this space" (it grinds his gears, said so in the very first post)
- AI shop talk: "agentic", "orchestrate", "context window", "LLM" (say "AI" or name the tool)
- Emojis and icon bullets

## His chat voice (for reference, not for posts)

When he writes quickly he's direct and a bit loose: "Lets just keep it as the E logo",
"I let you decide", "sorta". Posts are tidier than that, but keep the same directness:
say what you want, say why in a few words, move on.

## Checklist before showing a draft

1. Could a stranger follow it without knowing the project? If not, add one plain line.
2. Is every claim either measured, built, or flagged as not yet tried?
3. Any word from the avoid list? Any sentence over about 25 words? Cut it.
4. Credits and links to the projects it builds on are in.
5. Ends with what's next and `-SM`.
